# Test 1: wavelength-axis calibration audit

## Evidence found in the archive
1. Annexure-I, ch3_lib_pds_data_user_manual.pdf - the spectrometric calibration:
   lambda(p) = 163.9731 + 0.38172 p - 1.48482e-5 p^2 - 2.17022e-9 p^3, p = 1..2094.
   Derived on ground from a calibrated Hg-Ar lamp (200-800 nm) by peak-centroid
   regression. This is the only documented dispersion solution in the bundle.
2. The parent L1 header reproduces it to 0.005 nm (the CSV's 2-decimal rounding).
3. miscellaneous/ch3_lib_pds_cal_res.csv - the radiometric transfer function,
   1,685 rows, 219.758-800.106 nm. Its wavelength column aligns to pixels 147-1832
   (exactly the "preferable" range stated in Annexure-II) and agrees with the
   Annexure-I polynomial to a maximum of 0.0005 nm, its own 3-decimal rounding.
   This product was generated by a SEPARATE calibration process (deuterium-halogen
   lamp radiometry), so it is an independent internal check, not a restatement.
4. Annexure-II - base-temperature pixel-shift correction, <1/6 pixel per degree C
   relative to 25 C, with an empirical equation. NOT applied in the L1 product.
5. Application Note-2 - working-distance (WD) wavelength-shift correction relative
   to a nominal 205 mm, with an empirical equation and five reference peak-wavelength
   markers (251.75, 288.32, 394.55, 396.88, 777.28 nm) and a +/-0.17 nm criterion.
6. Instrument specs (SIS, user manual, payload note): range 220-800 nm, resolution
   "less than a nm", 2048-pixel linear CCD, ITSD 205 +/- 10 mm (hard surfaces),
   205 +/- 6 mm (powders).

## Can the parent axis be independently corroborated within the archive?
Yes, internally and at the level of the dispersion solution. Three archive sources
agree: the Annexure-I polynomial, the parent L1 header, and the independently
produced radiometric calibration table. Maximum disagreement 0.005 nm.

The single-shot subfile axis disagrees with the radiometric table by 7.47-8.90 nm
and matches no documented transformation. It is contradicted by all three sources.
It is not a pixel shift of the polynomial (best integer offset leaves 2.04 nm
residual) and is not describable by any cubic in pixel index (1.21 nm residual).
Its offset profile (11.1 nm at the blue end, ~7.5 nm mid-band, 5.0 nm at the red
end) does not match the shape of the WD correction term either, so "the subfile
axis is a WD-corrected axis" is not supported.

One internal contradiction remains: page 8 of the user manual diagrams the L1
file layout with a payload header reading 153.21 ... 873.3, i.e. the subfile axis.
Three independent products outweigh one figure, but the contradiction should be
reported and referred to the archive provider (contacts in readme.txt).

## What is NOT documented anywhere
- Any quoted wavelength accuracy or uncertainty for the L1 axis.
- Any air/vacuum convention. The ground calibration used a Hg-Ar lamp in a
  laboratory, which conventionally implies air above 200 nm, but no document
  states it. Air-vacuum differs by 0.075 nm at 250 nm and 0.220 nm at 800 nm.
- Any relationship between the parent and single-shot axes.
- The working distance actually achieved during any rover operation. Application
  Note-2 states explicitly that no direct WD measurement exists.

## Wavelength uncertainty budget (per observed peak, parent axis)
  dispersion solution, internal consistency          <= 0.005 nm
  air/vacuum convention unknown                       0.075 - 0.220 nm
  temperature term, Annexure-II, NOT applied in L1    0.77 - 0.95 nm
      (worked example: base temperature 8.95 C, dT = -16.05 C)
  working-distance term, Application Note-2, D unknown
      sensitivity up to 0.749 nm per mm at 417 nm
      +/- 6 mm  (powder ITSD spec)  -> up to +/- 4.5 nm near 400 nm
      +/- 10 mm (hard-surface spec) -> up to +/- 7.4 nm near 400 nm
      strongly wavelength dependent: ~0.1 nm/mm at 250 nm, ~0.03 nm/mm at 777 nm

The dominant term is working distance and it is unbounded by any measurement.
Fitting D to the five markers on the worked example gives a best RMS of 0.87 nm
with only 1 of 5 markers inside the archive's own +/-0.17 nm criterion, and an
implied WD of 184.7 mm, outside the 205 +/- 10 mm specification. The marker test
therefore does not close, and the residual is not explained by temperature and
working distance alone.

## Defensible statement
The L1 parent-table wavelength axis is the archive's documented spectrometric
calibration and is corroborated by an independently generated calibration product
to better than 0.01 nm. The physical wavelength of an observed emission peak is
nevertheless uncertain at the ~1 nm level from the unapplied temperature
correction alone, and by several nm in the 300-500 nm region once the unmeasured
working distance is admitted. This uncertainty exceeds the +/-0.5 nm matching
tolerance used for candidate annotation.

## What remains unknown
Whether L1 wavelengths are air or vacuum; the working distance of every operation;
the provenance of the single-shot axis; and whether residual error beyond
temperature and working distance exists, which the failed marker test suggests.
